# Imagery Foundation

Kingfisher imagery is restrained, atmospheric, and specific. It should support orientation and identity without turning every screen into an illustration.

## 1. Canonical visual motifs

- the kingfisher bird
- water and reflective surfaces
- quiet Central-European landscape language
- branches/perches as calm structural anchors
- flight paths and curved relationship lines
- geometric information fragments organising into clarity
- soft atmospheric depth rather than glossy 3D abstraction

## 2. Bird rendering

The canonical bird should feel recognisably like a real kingfisher while retaining enough editorial control to function as a brand asset.

Target character:
- anatomically recognisable kingfisher
- elegant, not cute
- detailed but not photorealistic stock photography
- controlled blue/teal upper plumage and copper/orange underside
- crisp silhouette at small sizes
- natural posture and motion
- consistent lighting and proportions across state assets

The bird must not drift into:
- cartoon mascot
- fantasy creature
- generic blue bird
- hyper-saturated neon illustration
- inconsistent anatomy between screens

## 3. Environmental scenes

Environmental imagery should be used for major emotional surfaces such as Today and Morning Briefing.

Characteristics:
- quiet natural light
- subtle fog/haze where appropriate
- water, hills, landscape, or restrained local context
- generous negative space for UI
- no busy stock-photo compositions
- no artificial cyberpunk styling

## 4. Asset consistency

Character assets should be generated or illustrated from the same canonical master reference. New state assets must preserve the same bird identity.

## 5. Coding-agent rule

Coding agents may position, crop, mask, or responsively scale approved images. They may not regenerate, redraw, trace approximately, or substitute characteristic Kingfisher imagery.
